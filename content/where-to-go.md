It is often said that a culture is either individualistic or collectivistic. Put another way, that there is a distinction between the interest of the individual and the collective. This idea supposes the existence of an individual without the collective, along with the implication that the collective is one homogenous mass with no differing layers of class interests. In modern society, this is completely wrong. The collective, along with society itself, is nothing other than the sum of material and social relations that are made between individuals.

If that is true, then why does life often feel antagonistic to individuals, to myself? Why has the interconnectedness of the world, created by the wonderous capitalism, become the very creator of this dead weight that I feel everyday?

This pressure forces us to come to conclusions not only of what life is, but what to do to escape. "How do I live a worthwhile life?", that is, how will can I get life before death? Understandably, many choose to dedicate their lives to themselves, to their narrow existence, a mindset of, "I shall fight for my share". Looking around and looking at life empirically, one cannot see the possibilities created by the class contradictions in society. All they can see is more of the same society.

However, *no amount of personal wealth* will ever change the fact that this society produces the conditions for the very same misery that we work everyday to escape. It does not matter how many zeros you have in your bank account or the cult gathering you may amass if at any moment the priviledges that you hold can be swept away, by the crises that have only become more and more common in modern society.  

I don't see the need anymore to contemplate my identity or pride, the purpose of life, or to worry about my current mood. These are all secondary, or even tertiary concerns that will only be washed away with time. I want to live a life rich in content, not in form. I want to walk on the beach without feeling the dread for the next week of meaningless work, a society without oppression and poverty. If that means I must sacrafice part of my life to realize this project, then that must be accepted as a given.

Now, we shall call moving on and personal growth the realization that the old way of thinking was not an unhappy coincidence or fit of madness, but merely the vacillations caused by a completely false set of questions: that neither the answers yes or no could solve a problem that would not exist by the time it really mattered.
