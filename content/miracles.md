<!-- This is a reflection about content. -->

1. Pushing beyond the pacificism and emptiness of today's society is difficult. Cultural moments and global events likewise come and go on a weekly basis, every declaration of economic disaster dissipates immediately like salt in water, and all the visible resistance comes in the form of moralism or nihilism.

2. We cannot only understand our lives as our experiences. That the meanings of our existence is merely defined by how we have felt, what we have done concretely, or how much accolades we have attained. We must move ourselves from the aether back into our place, the social relations that have produced us. To understand someone, we must treat them like a historical actor and subject.

3. Self-control is a meaningless phrase. It tells us nothing about lives if we are concerned with their content: It fails to be an ethics or an explanation.

4. (In the same way, assigning blame for *punishment* in most situations represents the exact same tendency)

5. Despite all the advances in their fields, no psychologist, psychiatrist or therapist has ever been able to find a technique to consistently make a person in poverty happy. That is not to say its impossible, they can be excused because they probably couldn't afford their services in the first place. Of course, bourgeois analyists outdo themselves by arguing that widespread despair comes from the feeling of the meaningless of life rather than poverty. That is, ideas come out of another idea. (and so on!)

6. (Observation) Today's young people aged 15-25 seem to be embarrassed of their own existence.

7. I want my life to be wealthy, but I feel the higher need for my life to *make sense*. I want to exist in a society where personal activity is understood as a social act and not an individual one.

8. I want to be a different person.

9. Despite everything, I still believe that miracles exist. That is, that my life and therefore *the entire world* can change.
